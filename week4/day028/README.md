## Rica Etme — Making Requests

### 1. Grammar: Making a Request with the Simple Present Question Form

The **question form of the simple present tense (Geniş Zaman)** can be used to make a polite **request**.

### Structure

**Verb stem + -(A/I)r + mI + personal ending?**

Examples:

| Turkish                                         | English                                                       |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Masayı siler misin?                             | Could you wipe the table?                                     |
| Silerim.                                        | Sure, I will.                                                 |
| Pencereyi açar mısın?                           | Could you open the window?                                    |
| Tabii.                                          | Of course.                                                    |
| Bu kitapları Fatih’e verir misin?               | Could you give these books to Fatih?                          |
| Evet, veririm.                                  | Yes, I will.                                                  |
| Çantamı tutar mısın?                            | Could you hold my bag?                                        |
| Şoför Bey, lütfen orta kapıyı açar mısınız?     | Sir, could you please open the middle door?                   |
| Bebek uyuyor. Müziğin sesini biraz kısar mısın? | The baby is sleeping. Could you turn the music down a little? |

### Important

**-mısın? / -misin? / -musun? / -müsün?**

These forms are very common when asking someone to do something.

Examples:

* **Gelir misin?** → Could you come?
* **Bakar mısın?** → Could you look?
* **Yardım eder misin?** → Could you help?
* **Açar mısın?** → Could you open it?
* **Verir misin?** → Could you give it?

For a more polite/formal request:

* **Açar mısınız?** → Could you open it?
* **Yardım eder misiniz?** → Could you help?
* **Bekler misiniz?** → Could you wait?

## MANAVDA — At the Greengrocer

**Semra Hanım:** İki kilo domates **verir misiniz?**

# SOKAKTA — On the Street

**İhtiyar:** Delikanlı **yardım eder misin?** Bu paketler çok ağır; benim için biraz **taşır mısın?** Evim şurada.
→ Young man, could you help me? These packages are very heavy; could you carry them for me? My house is right there.

**Genç:** Tabii amca, taşırım.
→ Of course, uncle. I'll carry them.

**İhtiyar:** Sağ ol evladım. İşte geldik. Benim gözlerim iyi görmüyor. Rica etsem kapıyı **açar mısın?** Anahtarlar burada.
→ Thank you, my son. Here we are. My eyesight isn't very good. If I may ask, could you open the door? The keys are here.

**Genç:** İşte, tamam.
→ There you go, okay.

**İhtiyar:** Çok teşekkür ederim evladım.
→ Thank you very much, my son.

### Vocabulary

* **ihtiyar** → old man / elderly person
* **delikanlı** → young man
* **ağır** → heavy
* **taşımak** → to carry
* **evladım** → my son / my child
* **gözlerim iyi görmüyor** → I can't see well
* **rica etsem** → if I may ask / if you don't mind

# ÇALIKUŞU — The Wren

**Çalıkuşu** literally means **“wren”**, a small bird.

It is also the title of a famous Turkish novel by **Reşat Nuri Güntekin**.

### Simplified English Summary

Feride is born in Istanbul. She loses her mother and father when she is young. Her aunt raises her.

Feride attends a French boarding school. She is very naughty and active, so her friends nickname her **“Çalıkuşu” (The Wren)** because she is as energetic as wrens.

Feride spends her summer holidays at her aunt's mansion. She and her aunt's son, Kâmran, fall in love and become engaged.

On their wedding day, a woman gives Feride a letter saying that Kâmran has a lover in Switzerland. Feride reads the letter, leaves Kâmran, and runs away.

She works as a teacher in different parts of Anatolia. Various rumors spread about the young and beautiful Feride.

She meets Doctor Hayrullah Bey. He protects Feride like a daughter. Because of rumors among the local people, he formally marries her. However, their relationship is only like that of a father and daughter.

Feride keeps a diary and writes everything in it. Hayrullah Bey finds the diary, reads it, and keeps it.

Later, Hayrullah Bey becomes ill. He tells Feride:

**“After I die, go to your aunt and give this envelope to Kâmran.”**

After his death, Feride goes to her aunt's house and gives Kâmran the envelope.

Inside are Hayrullah Bey's letter and Feride's diary.

Hayrullah Bey writes to Kâmran:

**“Feride'yi bir daha bırakma.”**
→ **“Don't leave Feride again.”**

Kâmran reads the letter and Feride's diary until morning. He learns everything.

He no longer leaves Feride, and they get married.

# 📚 -DIr Suffix

## Meaning

The suffix **-DIr** has several important uses.

At this level, one very important use is expressing **probability / assumption / inference**.

### Examples

**Babam şimdi çalışıyordur.**
→ My father is probably working now.

**Telefonu kapalı. Şimdi toplantıdadır.**
→ His phone is off. He is probably in a meeting now.

**Bence yarın toplantı olmayacaktır.**
→ I don't think there will be a meeting tomorrow.

**Ahmet şimdi yoldadır.**
→ Ahmet is probably on the way now.

**Antalya bu mevsimde çok sıcaktır.**
→ Antalya is very hot during this season.

---

# Important Pattern

### Verb + -DIr

With different tenses:

**-(I)yor + dur**

> çalışıyor + dur → **çalışıyordur**

**-(y)AcAk + tır**

> gelecek + tir → **gelecektir**

### Noun/adjective + -DIr

> evde + dir → **evdedir**

> sıcak + tır → **sıcaktır**

---

# -DIr = “probably / must be / is likely to”

Compare:

**Babam çalışıyor.**
→ My father is working.

**Babam çalışıyordur.**
→ My father is probably working.

The **-DIr** adds the speaker's inference/assumption.

# 🧠 Quick Mental Model

### **Rica etmek = Request**

When you want someone to do something:

> **Verb + -(A/I)r + misin/mısın/musun/müsün?**

**Gelir misin?** → Could you come?
**Bakar mısın?** → Could you look?
**Verir misin?** → Could you give me...?
**Açar mısın?** → Could you open...?
**Yapar mısın?** → Could you do...?

More formal:

> **Verb + -(A/I)r + misiniz/mısınız/musunuz/müsünüz?**

**Yardım eder misiniz?** → Could you help me?
**Bekler misiniz?** → Could you wait?
**Açar mısınız?** → Could you open it?

### **-DIr = probably / inference**

> **çalışıyor → çalışıyordur**
> He is working → He is probably working.

> **gelmiş → gelmiştir**
> He came/has come → He has probably come.

> **gelecek → gelecektir**
> He will come → He will probably come.

> **evde → evdedir**
> He is at home → He is probably at home.

**Core idea:**

> **Rica = “Could you...?”**
> **-DIr = “probably / most likely”**
