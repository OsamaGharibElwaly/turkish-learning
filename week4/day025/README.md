# 1. Gelecek Zaman — Future Tense

### 🇹🇷 What is it?

**Gelecek zaman** describes an action that **will happen in the future**.

English:

> I will go.
> She will study.
> We will travel.

Turkish:

> Gideceğim.
> Çalışacak.
> Gezeceğiz.

### Formula

**Verb stem + -(y)AcAk + personal ending**

| Person | Gitmek → Future |
| ------ | --------------- |
| Ben    | gideceğim       |
| Sen    | gideceksin      |
| O      | gidecek         |
| Biz    | gideceğiz       |
| Siz    | gideceksiniz    |
| Onlar  | gidecekler      |

### Why **gideceğim**, not *gitceğim*?

The verb is:

> git-mek

Remove **-mek**:

> git-

Add future:

> git + ecek

But Turkish has a consonant change:

> git + ecek → **gidecek**

Then personal ending:

> gidecek + im → **gideceğim**

---

### Example

> Eda yarın Antalya’ya **gidecek**.

**Eda will go to Antalya tomorrow.**

> Antalya’nın ilçesi Alanya’ya **yerleşecek**.

**She will settle in Alanya.**

> Orada suya **girecek**.

**She will go swimming / enter the water there.**

> Akşam yemeğini **yiyecek**.

**She will eat dinner.**

# 3. Kadar — As much as / As ... as

**kadar** compares quantity, degree, or extent.

### Noun + kadar

> Senin **kadar** uzun değilim.

I'm not as tall **as you**.

> Ayşe su **kadar** para harcıyor.

Ayşe spends **as much money as water**.

### Common pattern

> **X kadar + adjective**

> Senin kadar hızlı değilim.

I'm not as fast as you.

> Onun kadar zengin değil.

He/she isn't as rich as him/her.

# 6. The Most Important Grammar: -mIş

## Belirsiz Geçmiş Zaman

### Reported / Inferred Past Tense

This is one of the most important concepts in this unit.

**-mIş** is used when:

1. You **heard** information from somebody.
2. You **learned** something afterward.
3. You **didn't directly witness** the event.
4. You **realized/discovered** something afterward.
5. You are telling a **story/fairy tale**.

The Turkish textbook defines it as:

> Başka bir kişiden duyulan, öğrenilen veya sonradan farkına varılan bir olayı anlatmak.

**An event that was heard from another person, learned later, or noticed afterward.**

---

# 7. -DI vs -mIş

This distinction is extremely important.

### -DI = I directly know/witnessed it

> Dün Ahmet'i **gördüm**.

I saw Ahmet yesterday.

I personally saw him.

### -mIş = I heard/learned it

> Ahmet dün İstanbul'a **gitmiş**.

Apparently / I heard that Ahmet went to Istanbul.

I did not necessarily witness it.

---

## Compare

> Ahmet dün evlen**di**.

I know / witnessed that Ahmet got married.

vs.

> Ahmet dün evlen**miş**.

I heard / learned that Ahmet got married.

---

# 8. How to Build -mIş

### Positive

**Verb stem + mIş + personal ending**

For **unutmak**:

| Person | Turkish      | English                  |
| ------ | ------------ | ------------------------ |
| Ben    | unutmuşum    | apparently I forgot      |
| Sen    | unutmuşsun   | apparently you forgot    |
| O      | unutmuş      | apparently he/she forgot |
| Biz    | unutmuşuz    | apparently we forgot     |
| Siz    | unutmuşsunuz | apparently you forgot    |
| Onlar  | unutmuşlar   | apparently they forgot   |

### Vowel harmony

The vowel changes:

**-mış / -miş / -muş / -müş**

Examples:

> yapmış
> gelmiş
> bulmuş
> görmüş

---

# 9. Negative -mIş

Negative:

**verb stem + mA + mIş**

Examples:

> gelmemiş

He/she apparently didn't come.

> yapmamış

He/she apparently didn't do it.

> gitmemiş

He/she apparently didn't go.

> unutmadım

I didn't forget. **(-DI)**

> unutmamışım

Apparently I didn't forget.

---

# 10. -mIş Questions

Question particle:

> **mI**

Examples:

> Ali gelmiş mi?

Has Ali apparently come?

> Bebek uyumuş mu?

Did the baby apparently sleep?

> Antalya'ya gitmiş mi?

Has he/she apparently gone to Antalya?

### Negative question

> Gelmemiş mi?

Didn't he/she come?

---

# 11. Very Important: -mIş Can Mean "Apparently"

This is not always simply "past."

For example:

> Telefonumu arkadaşımın dükkânında **unutmuşum**.

**Apparently, I left my phone at my friend's shop.**

Maybe you just discovered it.

You didn't consciously witness yourself "forgetting."

That's why Turkish uses **-mIş**.

---

# 12. Nominal Sentences + -mIş

You can also use **-mIş** with adjectives and nouns.

Example:

> Gökçe eskiden çok tembel bir öğrenci**ymiş**.

Apparently Gökçe used to be a very lazy student.

Here there isn't a verb like *gitmek*.

The sentence is:

> Gökçe + tembel öğrenci + **ymiş**

The **y** is a buffer consonant.

# 13. Negative Nominal Sentences

Negative uses:

> **değil + miş**

Examples:

> hasta değilmiş

Apparently he/she wasn't sick.

> zengin değilmiş

Apparently he/she wasn't rich.

> öğrenci değilmiş

Apparently he/she wasn't a student.

### Compare

> O hasta**ydı**.

He/she was sick.
**Directly known past**

> O hasta**ymış**.

Apparently he/she was sick.
**Reported/inferred**

> O hasta **değilmiş**.

Apparently he/she wasn't sick.

---

# 14. -DI or -mIş? The Logic

Use this mental rule:

### 👀 I directly experienced it → -DI

> Dün yağmur yağ**dı**.

I experienced/saw the rain.

### 👂 I heard it → -mIş

> Dün yağmur yağ**mış**.

I heard that it rained.

### 🧠 I discovered it afterward → -mIş

> Cüzdanımı evde unut**muşum**.

Apparently I left my wallet at home.

### 📖 Story / fairy tale → often -mIş

> Bir varmış, bir yokmuş.

Once upon a time...

# 18. Reported Past in Stories

This is why fairy tales repeatedly use **-mIş**.

### Example

> Bir varmış, bir yokmuş.

Literally:

> There apparently was one, there apparently wasn't one.

Natural English:

> Once upon a time...

Then:

> Keloğlan'ın yaşlı bir annesi **varmış**.

Keloğlan apparently had an old mother.

> Bir gün Keloğlan balık tutmaya **gitmiş**.

One day, Keloğlan went fishing.

> Büyük bir balık **tutmuş**.

He caught a big fish.

This creates the **storytelling / legendary** feeling.

# 19. Rüya — Dream

To transform a direct narrative into reported past, change the verbs:

> kalktı → **kalkmış**
> başladı → **başlamış**
> çalıştı → **çalışmış**
> acıktı → **acıkmış**
> düşündü → **düşünmüş**
> yedi → **yemiş**
> uyudu → **uyumuş**
> gördü → **görmüş**
> verdi → **vermiş**
> dedi → **demiş**
> uyandı → **uyanmış**
> baktı → **bakmış**
> kapadı → **kapamış**

### Example transformation

Original:

> Nasreddin Hoca bir gün çok erken kalktı.

Reported:

> Nasreddin Hoca bir gün çok erken **kalkmış**.

Original:

> Öğlen karnı acıktı.

Reported:

> Öğlen karnı **acıkmış**.

# 22. The Logic Behind Pekiştirme

The textbook says Turkish inserts one of:

> **m / p / r / s**

before the adjective.

But don't try to derive every form yourself.

Many forms are **lexicalized**, so memorize common ones as vocabulary:

> güzel → **güpgüzel**
> temiz → **tertemiz**
> sarı → **sapsarı**
> mavi → **masmavi**

Think of them as special vocabulary forms.

# 25. -CIK

**-cık / -cik / -cuk / -cük**

can express:

* smallness
* affection
* cuteness
* pity

### Examples

> ufak → **ufacık**

tiny

> küçük → **küçücük**

very small / tiny

> çocuk → **çocukçuk**

poor little child

> kedi → **kedicik**

little/cute cat

---

### Affection

> anne → **anneciğim**

my dear mother

> baba → **babacığım**

my dear father

> arkadaş → **arkadaşçığım**

my dear friend

---

### Pity

> Zavallı **kedicik** çok hasta.

The poor little cat is very sick.

---

# 26. Important K → ∅ Change

When a word ending in **k** takes **-cık**, the final **k** can disappear.

> küçük + cük → **küçücük**

> ufak + cık → **ufacık**

> minik + cik → **minicik**

So don't mechanically write:

❌ küçükkük

Instead:

✅ **küçücük**

## 🎯 What you should actually master from this unit

If you're studying this for **A2 Turkish**, don't try to memorize the entire textbook. Make these **5 distinctions automatic**:

1. **-DI = I directly know/experienced it**

   * `Gördüm.`

2. **-mIş = I heard/discovered/inferred it**

   * `Görmüş.`

3. **-(y)AcAk = future**

   * `Göreceğim.`

4. **gibi = like**

   * `Tilki gibi kurnaz.`

5. **kadar = as ... as / as much as**

   * `Senin kadar hızlı.`

And then learn **common intensified adjectives as vocabulary**, rather than trying to calculate every `m/p/r/s` form from scratch.
