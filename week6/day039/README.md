# Turkish–English Learning: Yoğurdun Steve Jobs'u

We’ll study the text step by step: Turkish vocabulary → English translation → grammar → reading comprehension. I’ll keep the material easy to copy into your notes.

## 1. Title and main idea

Yoğurdun Steve Jobs'u

- yoğurt = yogurt
- -un = of the (possessive/genitive suffix here)
- Steve Jobs'u = the Steve Jobs of...

Natural English title: “The Steve Jobs of Yogurt.”

The text is an interview with Hamdi Ulukaya, a Turkish entrepreneur who founded Chobani, a well-known yogurt company in the United States.

## 2. First paragraph — Turkish and English

TURKISH

Dergilerin ve gazetelerin ekonomi sayfalarındaki başarı öykülerini aklınıza getirin.

ENGLISH

Think of the success stories on the business pages of magazines and newspapers.

TURKISH

Şık ofislerindeki büyük masalarının başındaki ya da fabrikalarının önündeki iş adamlarını ve iş kadınlarını mutlaka anımsayacaksınız.

ENGLISH

You will surely remember the businessmen and businesswomen sitting at their large desks in stylish offices or standing in front of their factories.

TURKISH

Hamdi Ulukaya (39) da onlardan biri.

ENGLISH

Hamdi Ulukaya (39) is one of them, too.

TURKISH

Üstelik başarı hikâyesi çok daha renkli.

ENGLISH

Moreover, his success story is much more colorful (interesting and unusual).

TURKISH

O, dedesi ve babası ile yaz aylarında yaylaya çıkıp koyun güdüyordu ve o zamanlar, hayatı hakkında hiçbir şey bilmiyordu.

ENGLISH

During the summer months, he used to go up to the highlands with his grandfather and father and herd sheep. Back then, he knew nothing about his future life.

TURKISH

Amerika'yı merak edip gitti, şimdi Amerika'nın en başarılı 10 iş adamından biri ve 40 yaş altındaki iş adamları arasında en parlak girişimci olarak anılıyor.

ENGLISH

Curious about America, he went there. Now he is one of America's ten most successful businessmen and is regarded as the most promising entrepreneur among businessmen under 40.

## 4. Grammar you should learn

### A. The past continuous: `-yordu`

Koyun güdüyordu. = He was herding sheep / He used to herd sheep.

This form describes an ongoing or habitual past action.

| Infinitive | Past continuous | English            |
| ---------- | --------------- | ------------------ |
| çalışmak   | çalışıyordu     | was working        |
| gitmek     | gidiyordu       | was going          |
| yapmak     | yapıyordu       | was doing          |
| bilmek     | biliyordu       | knew / was knowing |
| gütmek     | güdüyordu       | was herding        |

Remember: Depending on context, `-yordu` can mean was doing or used to do.

### B. The suffix `-ıp / -ip / -up / -üp`

Yaylaya çıkıp koyun güdüyordu.

\= He would go up to the highlands and herd sheep.

This suffix connects actions performed in sequence.

- Eve gidip uyudum. = I went home and slept.
- Kahvaltı yapıp işe gittim. = I had breakfast and went to work.
- Kitabı alıp çıktım. = I took the book and left.

### C. `-dan biri` = one of ...

Amerika'nın en başarılı 10 iş adamından biri.

\= One of America's ten most successful businessmen.

More examples:

- En iyi öğrencilerden biri. = One of the best students.
- Arkadaşlarımdan biri. = One of my friends.
- Dünyanın en büyük şirketlerinden biri. = One of the world's biggest companies.

## 3. Grammar — Noun-forming suffix `-(y)Iş`

Turkish: İsim-Fiil Ekleri: `-(y)ış, -(y)iş, -(y)uş, -(y)üş`

English: Verb-to-noun suffixes, especially describing the manner or way an action is performed.

This suffix turns a verb into a noun. It can describe a way of doing something, an action, or the occurrence of an event.

### Formation

Verb stem + (y)Iş + possessive suffix (when needed)

The capital `I` represents a vowel that changes according to Turkish vowel harmony: `ı, i, u, ü`.

| Verb      | Noun      | English                                                      |
| --------- | --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| bakmak    | bakış     | look / gaze                                                  |
| görmek    | görüş     | view / opinion                                               |
| gitmek    | gidiş     | departure / going                                            |
| dönmek    | dönüş     | return                                                       |
| dikmek    | dikiş     | sewing / stitch                                              |
| gülmek    | gülüş     | smile / way of laughing                                      |
| yürümek   | yürüyüş   | walk / walking                                               |
| davranmak | davranış  | behavior                                                     |
| anlaşmak  | anlaşış\* | not normally used with this meaning                          |
| çalışmak  | çalışış\* | not normally used in ordinary conversation with this meaning |

\\\*Not every verb naturally forms a commonly used noun with this suffix. Learn the established words rather than assuming every combination is correct.

### Examples from the lesson

1\. Onun bakışlarından hiç hoşlanmıyorum.

- onun = his / her
- bakışlarından = from his/her looks
- hiç hoşlanmıyorum = I don't like at all

Natural translation: I don't like the way he/she looks at me at all.

2\. Yeni programdaki değişiklikler için herkesin görüşünü almak lazım.

- yeni programdaki = in the new program
- değişiklikler = changes
- herkesin = everyone's
- görüşünü = opinion
- almak lazım = it is necessary to get

Translation: We need to get everyone's opinion about the changes in the new program.

3\. Ankara'ya bir gidiş dönüş bileti lütfen!

Translation: A round-trip ticket to Ankara, please!

- gidiş = going / outbound journey
- dönüş = return
- gidiş dönüş = round trip

4\. Annem gençliğinde dikiş nakış kursuna gitmiş.

Translation: My mother attended a sewing and embroidery course when she was young.

- gençliğinde = when she was young
- dikiş = sewing
- nakış = embroidery
- kurs = course
- gitmiş = apparently went / attended

### Important distinction

Compare these forms:

- gitmek = to go (verb)
- gidiş = going / departure (noun)
- gidişi = his/her departure or the way he/she goes, depending on context
- gidiş dönüş = round trip

The suffix changes a verb into a noun, and a possessive suffix can then indicate whose action or manner it is.

# Turkish–English Learning — Meslekler (Professions)

## 1. Grammar: `bitirmemi söyledi`, `olmamı istedi`, `yapmasını rica ettim`

This structure is used when one person tells, wants, or asks another person to do something.

### The pattern

Person + verb noun (`-ma/-me`) + possessive suffix + reporting verb

| Turkish                                          | English                                            |
| ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Müdür işlerimi yarına kadar bitirmemi söyledi.   | The manager told me to finish my work by tomorrow. |
| Babam benim doktor olmamı istiyordu.             | My father wanted me to become a doctor.            |
| Ben ona bu akşam yemekleri yapmasını rica ettim. | I asked him/her to make the meals this evening.    |

A more natural Turkish version of the last sentence is: Ben ondan bu akşam yemekleri yapmasını rica ettim. = I asked him/her to prepare the meals this evening.

### How the grammar works

| Form      | Meaning                      |
| --------- | ---------------------------- |
| bitirmek  | to finish                    |
| bitirmem  | my finishing                 |
| bitirmemi | that I finish / me to finish |
| olmak     | to be / become               |
| olmam     | my being / becoming          |
| olmamı    | that I be / become           |
| yapmak    | to do / make                 |
| yapması   | his/her doing                |
| yapmasını | that he/she do / make        |

Notice the difference:

- Benim bitirmem = my finishing.
- Bitirmemi söyledi = he/she told me to finish.
- Onun bitirmesi = his/her finishing.
- Bitirmesini istedi = he/she wanted him/her to finish.

### More examples

\- Öğretmen ödevimi yapmamı istedi. The teacher wanted me to do my homework.

\- Doktor daha fazla su içmemi söyledi. The doctor told me to drink more water.

\- Arkadaşımdan bana yardım etmesini rica ettim. I asked my friend to help me.

\- Annem erken yatmamı istiyor. My mother wants me to go to bed early.

# Turkish–English Learning: “Senden Bir Şey Olmaz”

## 1. Full English translation

“You'll Never Amount to Anything”

Many famous people in our country and around the world have a story hidden in their past in which someone told them, “You'll never amount to anything.” In their lives, this phrase has shed its meaning of humiliation and has almost turned into a magical phrase.

From music to sports, from theater to acting, from modeling to the business world, this phrase has opened the path to fame for many celebrities. Despite its negative meaning, it has also been extremely effective in helping them shine like stars. So much so that if they had never heard these words and had not become determined to prove themselves, perhaps we wouldn't know them today.

Sezen Aksu, the “Little Sparrow” of Turkish pop music, is now one of the most famous singers in the country. But how many copies of her 1975 single, Haydi Şansım, did she sell? Brace yourself: only 50 copies! In fact, people close to her were the ones who bought the album. Aksu left school and came from İzmir to Istanbul. Like many famous artists, she went from one record company to another in Unkapanı. However, every door was shut in her face as people told her, “You'll never become a singer.”

Even Walt Disney, creator of the world's most famous mouse and brand, heard these words. Before drawing the character Mickey Mouse and becoming famous, Walt Disney received rejection from five jobs, and an editor at a newspaper in America criticized him for lacking drawing ability. Before establishing Disneyland, he also went bankrupt several times, but eventually he won acceptance from the whole world.

Henry Ford, a pioneer of the automotive industry, applied to a bank for a loan in 1903 but was rejected. When he asked the manager, “How can you turn down such a big project?”, the bank manager replied, “An automobile may be just a temporary fashion.” Ford then said, “One day, automobiles will replace horse-drawn carriages on the roads.” Ford went bankrupt five times while trying to achieve his goal and faced countless obstacles.

## 3. Important expressions

### A. `Senden bir şey olmaz.`

Literal translation: “Nothing will come of you.”

Natural English equivalents:

- You'll never amount to anything.
- You'll never make it.
- You'll never succeed.

This is a negative judgment about someone's potential.

### B. `Hırs yapmak`

Meaning: To become determined to succeed, often because someone doubts you.

Örnek: Bana başarısız olacağımı söylediler ama hırs yaptım.

They told me I would fail, but I became determined to prove them wrong.

### C. `Bütün kapılar yüzüne kapandı.`

Meaning: Every door was shut in his/her face.

This is an idiom meaning that someone was rejected repeatedly or denied opportunities.

Örnek: İlk iş başvurularımda bütün kapılar yüzüme kapandı.

All doors were shut in my face during my first job applications.

### D. `Kendini kabul ettirmek`

Meaning: To prove oneself and earn acceptance or recognition.

Örnek: Çok çalışarak kendimi iş yerinde kabul ettirdim.

I worked hard and earned recognition at work.

### E. `Hedefine ulaşmak`

Meaning: To achieve one's goal.

Örnek: Hedefime ulaşmak için her gün çalışıyorum.

I work every day to achieve my goal.
