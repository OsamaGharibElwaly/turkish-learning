# 🇹🇷 The Ultimate Beginner’s Guide to Turkish Survival Phrases
*Based on the core essentials for your first conversations in Turkish.*

### 🔑 Quick Pronunciation Cheat Sheet
Before you start, know these 6 unique Turkish letters. Turkish is 100% phonetic (read exactly as written):
*   **ç** = "ch" (like **ch**air)
*   **ş** = "sh" (like **sh**oe)
*   **ı** = "uh" (like the 'a' in **a**bout)
*   **ö** = "eu" (like the 'eu' in E**ur**ope)
*   **ü** = "u" (like the French/German 'ü', round your lips and say "ee")
*   **ğ** = Silent. It just makes the vowel before it longer (e.g., *dağ* sounds like "daa").

## 1. Greetings & Introductions
Turkish strictly separates **Informal** (friends, family, people your age) and **Formal** (strangers, elders, shopkeepers, officials). When in doubt, **always use Formal**.

| Context | Turkish Phrase | Literal Translation | Pronunciation Tip |
| :--- | :--- | | :--- |
| **Informal Hello** | Selam. | Hi. | *Seh-lahm* |
| **Formal Hello** | Merhaba. | Hello. | *Mehr-hah-bah* |
| **I am [Name]** | Ben [Name]. | I [Name]. | Drop "Ben" if you want; the context is clear. |
| **Nice to meet you** | Tanıştığıma memnun oldum. | I became pleased to meet you. | *Tah-nish-tuh-ee-mah mem-noon ol-doom* |
| **Very nice to meet you**| **Çok** tanıştığıma memnun oldum. | *Very* pleased to meet you. | Adding **Çok** (very) makes it warmer and more polite. |

## 2. The Art of Being Polite (Thanks & Apologies)

### Saying "Thank You"
*   **Teşekkürler:** "Thanks" *(Casual, quick, universal)*
*   **Sağ ol:** "Thanks" *(Literally "Be healthy". Very common and friendly)*
*   **Teşekkür ederim:** "Thank you" *(Standard, polite)*
*   **Çok teşekkür ederim:** "Thank you very much" *(Formal and sincere)*

### Responding ("You're Welcome")
*   **Bir şey değil:** "It's nothing" / "Don't mention it."
*   **Sorun değil:** "No problem."
*   **Rica ederim:** "You're welcome" *(The most standard, polite response).*

### Apologizing & Getting Attention
*   **Afedersiniz:** "Excuse me" *(Formal. Use this to get a waiter's attention or ask a stranger a question).*
    *   *Example:* Afedersiniz, bir kahve lütfen. *(Excuse me, one coffee please.)*
*   **Pardon:** "Excuse me / Oops" *(Casual. Use if you bump into someone).*
*   **Özür dilerim:** "I am sorry" *(Use this for a genuine apology when you made a mistake).*

## 4. Survival Phrases: When You Need Help
Use these when you are lost or need to switch to English. 

*   **Do you speak English? (Informal):** İngilizce biliyor musun?
*   **Do you speak English? (Formal):** Afedersiniz, İngilizce biliyor musunuz?
*   **Yes:** Evet *(Eh-vet)*
*   **No:** Hayır *(Hi-yir)*
*   **I cannot speak English:** İngilizce konuşamıyorum.

> 💡 **Pro Tip:** You can swap out *İngilizce* (English) for any language:
> *   *İspanyolca* (Spanish)
> *   *Almanca* (German)
> *   *Türkçe* (Turkish) → *Türkçe konuşamıyorum* (I can't speak Turkish).

## 5. Numbers & Shopping
Turkish numbers are highly logical. Once you know 1–10 and the "tens," you can count to 100 easily.

### The Basics (1–10)
1. Bir | 2. İki | 3. Üç | 4. Dört | 5. Beş 
6. Altı | 7. Yedi | 8. Sekiz | 9. Dokuz | 10. On

### The Tens (10–100)
*   10: **On**
*   20: **Yirmi**
*   30: **Otuz**
*   40: **Kırk** *(Note the ı!)*
*   50: **Elli**
*   60: **Altmış**
*   70: **Yetmiş**
*   80: **Seksen**
*   90: **Doksan**
*   100: **Yüz**

### The Compound Rule (11–99)
Simply say the **Ten + Unit**, separated by a space. No "and" is needed.
*   25 = Yirmi (20) + beş (5) → **Yirmi beş**
*   56 = Elli (50) + altı (6) → **Elli altı**
*   98 = Doksan (90) + sekiz (8) → **Doksan sekiz**

### Asking for Prices
*   **Bu ne kadar?** = How much is *this*? (When holding it or it's right in front of you).
*   **O ne kadar?** = How much is *that*? (When pointing at something further away).
*   **Quick Formula:** `[Item Name] + ne kadar?`
    *   *Example:* Espresso ne kadar? *(How much is an espresso?)*
    *   *Example:* Bu çanta ne kadar? *(How much is this bag?)*

## 🚀 Beginner Action Plan
1. **Don't stress about the pronoun "Ben" (I).** In Turkish, the verb ending tells you who is speaking. Saying *"Çalışıyorum"* is perfectly complete and means "I am working."
2. **Master the Formal "Siz".** When talking to anyone you don't know, use the formal versions (*biliyor musunuz, teşekkür ederim*). Turks highly appreciate this respect.
3. **Practice the numbers out loud.** Turkish vowel harmony makes numbers flow beautifully once you get the rhythm.

*Save this guide, practice 3–5 phrases a day, and you will be ready for your first Turkish conversation!*
