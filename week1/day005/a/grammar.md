# 🇹🇷 Turkish Grammar: Demonstratives & Question Words

This topic covers **pointing at things** (this, that, these, those) and asking **who/what** questions in Turkish.

---

## 1. Demonstrative Pronouns (İşaret Zamirleri)

Turkish has **three levels of distance** for pointing at things:

| Turkish | English | Distance | Arabic |
|---------|---------|----------|--------|
| **Bu** | This | Close to speaker | هذا / هذه |
| **Şu** | That | Close to listener / nearby | ذلك / تلك |
| **O** | That (over there) | Far from both | ذلك / تلك (بعيد) |

### Examples:
- **Bu kalem.** → This is a pen. (in my hand)
- **Şu kalem.** → That is a pen. (near you)
- **O kalem.** → That is a pen. (far away)

> 💡 **Important:** Turkish has **NO gender** (no masculine/feminine distinction like in Arabic or Spanish). "Bu" works for both "this (masc.)" and "this (fem.)"

---

## 2. Question Words: Kim? & Ne?

| Turkish | English | Used For |
|---------|---------|----------|
| **Kim?** | Who? | People |
| **Ne?** | What? | Objects, places, things |

### Examples:
- **Bu ne?** → What is this?
  - *Bu kalem.* → This is a pen.

- **O kim?** → Who is that?
  - *O öğretmen.* → That is a teacher.

- **Şu ne?** → What is that (nearby)?
  - *Şu bilgisayar.* → That is a computer.

---

## 3. Plural Forms: bunlar, şunlar, onlar

To make demonstratives plural, we add **-lar/-ler**:

| Singular | Plural | English |
|----------|--------|---------|
| **bu** | **bunlar** | these |
| **şu** | **şunlar** | those (nearby) |
| **o** | **onlar** | those (far) |

### Examples:
- **Bunlar ne?** → What are these?
  - *Bunlar kitaplar.* → These are books.

- **Şunlar kim?** → Who are those (nearby)?
  - *Şunlar öğrenciler.* → Those are students.

- **Onlar ne?** → What are those (far)?
  - *Onlar köpekler.* → Those are dogs.

---

## 4. Place Demonstratives: burası, şurası, orası

When pointing at **places/locations**:

| Turkish | English |
|---------|---------|
| **Burası** | This place / Here |
| **Şurası** | That place / There (nearby) |
| **Orası** | That place / Over there (far) |

### Examples:
- **Burası neresi?** → What place is this?
  - *Burası sınıf.* → This is a classroom.

- **Şurası neresi?** → What place is that (nearby)?
  - *Şurası bahçe.* → That is a garden.

- **Orası neresi?** → What place is that (far)?
  - *Orası park.* → That is a park.

---

## 5. Plural Suffix: -lar / -ler

Turkish uses vowel harmony for plurals:

| Last Vowel | Plural Suffix | Example |
|------------|---------------|---------|
| **a, ı, o, u** | **-lar** | kitap → kitap**lar** (books) |
| **e, i, ö, ü** | **-ler** | kedi → kedi**ler** (cats) |

### More Examples:
- **kız** → kız**lar** (girls)
- **kutu** → kutu**lar** (boxes)
- **ev** → ev**ler** (houses)
- **göz** → göz**ler** (eyes)
- **üzüm** → üzüm**ler** (grapes)

---

## 6. The Optional Suffix: -(d)Ir

You might see sentences like:
- **Bu kalemdir.** = **Bu kalem.** (This is a pen.)
- **Bu öğretmendir.** = **Bu öğretmen.** (This is a teacher.)

The suffix **-(d)Ir** (dır/dir/dur/dür) means "is" but it's **optional** in Turkish. It's more formal and often omitted in spoken Turkish.

---

## 📝 Practice Examples

**Singular:**
- **Bu ne?** → Bu çanta. (What is this? → This is a bag.)
- **Şu kim?** → Şu polis. (Who is that? → That is a police officer.)
- **O ne?** → O telefon. (What is that over there? → That is a phone.)

**Plural:**
- **Bunlar ne?** → Bunlar defterler. (What are these? → These are notebooks.)
- **Şunlar kim?** → Şunlar öğretmenler. (Who are those? → Those are teachers.)
- **Onlar ne?** → Onlar köpekler. (What are those? → Those are dogs.)

---

## 🎯 Key Points to Remember

✅ **No gender** in Turkish (bu works for everything)  
✅ **Three distances**: bu (close), şu (medium), o (far)  
✅ **Kim?** for people, **Ne?** for objects  
✅ **Plural suffix**: -lar/-ler (follows vowel harmony)  
✅ **Place words**: burası, şurası, orası  
✅ **-(d)Ir** is optional and formal

Here is a complete, easy-to-understand guide to the Turkish question particle **mI**, explained in English with plenty of examples. 


# 🇹🇷 Turkish Grammar: The Question Particle "mI" (Soru Eki)

In English, we use words like **"do," "does," "is,"** or **"are"** to turn a statement into a Yes/No question. In Arabic, you use **"هل"**. 
In Turkish, there is no separate word for this. Instead, we add the **question particle "mI"** to the sentence.

### 📌 The Golden Rule
Even though it is called a "suffix" (eki), **it is always written as a separate word** in Turkish. 
* ❌ *Güzelmi?* (Wrong)
* ✅ *Güzel mi?* (Right)

---

### 1. The 4 Forms of "mI" (Vowel Harmony)
Because Turkish uses vowel harmony, "mI" changes its vowel to match the last vowel of the word it follows. It has 4 forms:

| Last Vowel of the Word | Question Particle | Example | Translation |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **a, ı** | **mı** | *Kitap **mı**?* | Is it a book? |
| **e, i** | **mi** | *Güzel **mi**?* | Is it beautiful? |
| **o, u** | **mu** | *Okul **mu**?* | Is it a school? |
| **ö, ü** | **mü** | *Gün **mü**?* | Is it a day? |

---

### 2. Adding Personal Suffixes (Am I? Are you? Is he?)
To ask "Am I...?" or "Are you...?", you add personal suffixes directly to **mI**. 

Here is the conjugation using the adjective *hasta* (sick) - *(Last vowel is 'a', so we use **mı**)*:

| Subject | Suffix | Turkish | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ben** (I) | -mıyım | *Hasta **mıyım**?* | Am I sick? |
| **Sen** (You - singular) | -mısın | *Hasta **mısın**?* | Are you sick? |
| **O** (He/She/It) | -mı | *Hasta **mı**?* | Is he/she sick? |
| **Biz** (We) | -mıyız | *Hasta **mıyız**?* | Are we sick? |
| **Siz** (You - plural/formal)| -mısınız | *Hasta **mısınız**?* | Are you (all) sick? |
| **Onlar** (They) | -mı | *Hasta **mı**?* | Are they sick? |

*(Note: For "They", we usually just use **mı**, but **mılar** is also occasionally used in spoken Turkish).*

**Let's look at a word ending in 'e/i' (using *öğrenci* - student):**
* *Öğrenci **miyim**?* (Am I a student?)
* *Öğrenci **misin**?* (Are you a student?)
* *Öğrenci **mi**?* (Is he/she a student?)

---

### 3. Changing the Focus of the Question
In Turkish, **the word right before "mI" is the focus of the question.** You can move "mI" around the sentence to ask about specific things.

**Statement:** *Ali okula gidiyor.* (Ali is going to school.)

* **Questioning the whole action:**
  * *Ali okula gidiyor **mu**?* 
  * (Is Ali going to school? -> Yes or No)

* **Questioning the subject (Is it ALI?):**
  * *Ali **mi** okula gidiyor?* 
  * (Is **Ali** the one going to school? -> No, Ayşe is.)

* **Questioning the destination (Is it TO SCHOOL?):**
  * *Ali okula **mı** gidiyor?* 
  * (Is Ali going **to school**? -> No, he is going to the park.)

---

### 4. Questions with "Where", "What", "Who" (Wh- Questions)
You can also use **mI** with question words like *Nerede* (Where), *Ne* (What), *Kim* (Who) to ask for specific information.

* *Bu **ne**?* (What is this?) ➡️ *Bu bir kalem **mi**?* (Is this a pen?)
* *Ali **nerede**?* (Where is Ali?) ➡️ *Ali evde **mi**?* (Is Ali at home?)
* *Bu **kimin**?* (Whose is this?) ➡️ *Bu senin kitabın **mı**?* (Is this your book?)

---

### 🗣️ Pronunciation Pro-Tip (Stress)
In Turkish statements, the stress is usually on the last syllable of the verb. But in Yes/No questions, **the stress shifts to the syllable right before "mI"**.

* Statement: *Güzel.* (Stress on *zel*)
* Question: *Gü**zel** mi?* (Stress heavily on *zel*, drop the pitch on *mi*)

* Statement: *Okulda.* (Stress on *da*)
* Question: *Okul**da** mı?* (Stress heavily on *da*)

---

### 📝 Quick Practice Quiz
Try to translate these in your head, then check the answers below:
1. Are you tired? *(yorgun)*
2. Is this a car? *(araba)*
3. Am I late? *(geç)*

**Answers:**
1. *Yorgun **musun**?* (yorgun ends in 'u' -> mu)
2. *Bu bir araba **mı**?* (araba ends in 'a' -> mı)
3. *Geç **miyim**?* (geç ends in 'e' -> mi)

# 🇷 Turkish Grammar: Pointing at Places (Demonstratives)

In Turkish, when we point at places or objects, we use specific words depending on the distance. This is similar to "This," "That," and "Over there" in English.

### 1. The Three "Places" (Yer Yöneltme Zamirleri)

| Turkish | English Meaning | Distance | Arabic Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Burası** | **This place / Here** | Very close to the speaker. | هذا المكان (هنا) |
| **Şurası** | **That place / There** | Close to the listener, or a short distance away. | ذلك المكان (هناك) |
| **Orası** | **That place / Over there** | Far away from both speaker and listener. | ذلك المكان (هناك بعيداً) |

---

### 2. Asking "What is this place?"

To ask about a location, we use the question word **Neresi?** (Which place? / Where?).

*   **Burası neresi?** ️ What is this place? (Here)
*   **Şurası neresi?** ➡️ What is that place? (There)
*   **Orası neresi?** ➡️ What is that place? (Over there)

**How to answer:**
You simply replace the question word with the name of the place.
*   *Burası neresi?* ➡️ **Burası ev.** (This place is a house.)
*   *Şurası neresi?* ️ **Şurası bahçe.** (That place is a garden.)
*   *Orası neresi?* ➡️ **Orası sokak.** (That place is a street.)

---

### 3. Asking Yes/No Questions (Is this place...?)

Remember the question particle **mI** (mı/mi/mu/mü) from the previous lesson? We use it here to ask "Is this place Ankara?"

*   **Burası Ankara mı?** ➡️ Is this place Ankara?
    *   *Evet, burası Ankara.* (Yes, this is Ankara.)
    *   *Hayır, burası Ankara **değil**, İstanbul.* (No, this place is **not** Ankara, it is Istanbul.)

*   **Şurası İzmir mi?** ➡️ Is that place İzmir?
    *   *Evet, şurası İzmir.* (Yes, that is İzmir.)
    *   *Hayır, Bursa.* (No, it's Bursa.)

> **Grammar Note:** The word **değil** means "not" or "is not". It is used to make negative sentences.
> *   *Burası okul.* (This is a school.) ➡️ *Burası okul **değil**.* (This is **not** a school.)

---
