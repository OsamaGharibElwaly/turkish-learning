# 🇹🇷 İstanbul A1 — Unit 5C

## **-DAn beri / -DIr = Since / For**

This is the next important time grammar after **önce / sonra**.

The main idea is:

> **Something started in the past and is still continuing now.**

English has two common forms:

* **since** → since 2020
* **for** → for 5 years

Turkish mainly uses:

> **-DAn beri**

and

> **-DIr**

---

# 1. -DAn beri — Since

**-DAn beri** means **since** a specific starting point.

### Formula

> **Starting time + -DAn + beri**

Examples:

> **2000'den beri**
> since 2000

> **sabahtan beri**
> since morning

> **pazartesiden beri**
> since Monday

> **geçen haftadan beri**
> since last week

### Sentences

> **2000'den beri yurtdışında yaşıyorum.**

I have been living abroad since 2000.

> **Sabahtan beri açım.**

I have been hungry since this morning.

> **Ekimden beri bu ofiste çalışıyoruz.**

We have been working in this office since October.

---

# 2. -DIr — For

**-DIr** is commonly used when talking about a **duration**.

> **beş aydır**

for five months

> **üç gündür**

for three days

> **yedi yıldır**

for seven years

> **iki saattir**

for two hours

### Examples

> **Üç gündür İstanbul'dayız.**

We have been in Istanbul for three days.

> **Beş aydır Türkçe öğreniyorum.**

I have been learning Turkish for five months.

> **Yedi yıldır Türkiye'de yaşıyorum.**

I have been living in Turkey for seven years.

---

# 3. The most important difference

Look at these:

### Since 2020

> **2020'den beri Türkçe öğreniyorum.**

I have been learning Turkish since 2020.

We know the **starting point** → 2020.

### For three years

> **Üç yıldır Türkçe öğreniyorum.**

I have been learning Turkish for three years.

We know the **duration**, not the exact starting date.

---

# 4. Very important textbook rule

Your book gives this rule:

### If the starting time is specified:

Use:

> **-DAn beri**

Example:

> **2000'den beri evliyim.**

I've been married since 2000.

You wouldn't normally say:

> ❌ 2000'dir evliyim.

because **2000** is a specific starting point.

---

### If the starting time isn't specified:

You can use either:

> **-DAn beri**

or

> **-DIr**

depending on the expression.

For example:

> **Sabahdan beri çalışıyorum.**

I've been working since morning.

You can also express a duration:

> **Üç saattir çalışıyorum.**

I've been working for three hours.

---

# 5. -DIr changes according to vowel harmony

You don't always say **-dır**.

It changes:

| Ending | Example  |
| ------ | -------- |
| -dır   | yıldır   |
| -dir   | gündür   |
| -dur   | aydır? ❌ |
| -dür   | süredir  |

Let's look at real examples:

> **üç gündür**

for three days

> **beş aydır**

for five months

> **iki yıldır**

for two years

> **iki saattir**

for two hours

> **üç haftadır**

for three weeks

So don't memorize only **-dır**.

Think:

> **-DIr = the 4-way vowel-harmony suffix**

---

# 6. Time words + -DIr

This is extremely common:

| Duration | Turkish      |
| -------- | ------------ |
| 1 day    | bir gündür   |
| 3 days   | üç gündür    |
| 1 week   | bir haftadır |
| 3 weeks  | üç haftadır  |
| 1 month  | bir aydır    |
| 5 months | beş aydır    |
| 1 year   | bir yıldır   |
| 7 years  | yedi yıldır  |
| 1 hour   | bir saattir  |
| 5 hours  | beş saattir  |

Examples:

> **Üç gündür hastayım.**

I've been sick for three days.

> **İki haftadır Türkçe çalışıyorum.**

I've been studying Turkish for two weeks.

> **Beş aydır İstanbul'da yaşıyorum.**

I've been living in Istanbul for five months.

---

# 7. "For" vs "Since"

This is probably the most important concept in Unit 5C.

### FOR

Duration:

> **Beş aydır Türkçe öğreniyorum.**

I have been learning Turkish **for five months**.

### SINCE

Starting point:

> **Ocaktan beri Türkçe öğreniyorum.**

I have been learning Turkish **since January**.

---

# 8. Compare these carefully

### ①

> **2019'dan beri İstanbul'da yaşıyorum.**

I've lived in Istanbul **since 2019**.

### ②

> **Yedi yıldır İstanbul'da yaşıyorum.**

I've lived in Istanbul **for seven years**.

They describe approximately the same idea, but the focus is different:

**2019'dan beri** → starting point

**yedi yıldır** → duration

---

# 9. "Ne zamandır?" — Since when / How long?

This is a very useful question.

> **Ne zamandır Türkçe öğreniyorsun?**

How long have you been learning Turkish?

Possible answers:

> **Üç aydır Türkçe öğreniyorum.**

I've been learning Turkish for three months.

OR:

> **Mayıstan beri Türkçe öğreniyorum.**

I've been learning Turkish since May.

---

# 10. Question: "How long?"

You can ask:

> **Kaç gündür?**

For how many days?

> **Kaç aydır?**

For how many months?

> **Kaç yıldır?**

For how many years?

> **Kaç saattir?**

For how many hours?

Examples:

> **Kaç yıldır Türkiye'de yaşıyorsun?**

How many years have you lived in Turkey?

> **Beş yıldır Türkiye'de yaşıyorum.**

I've lived in Turkey for five years.

---

# 11. Since morning

Your book has:

> **Sabahtan beri açım.**

I'm hungry since morning / I've been hungry since this morning.

**sabah + dan + beri**

> sabahtan beri

Another example:

> **Sabahtan beri çalışıyorum.**

I've been working since morning.

> **Sabahtan beri seni bekliyorum.**

I've been waiting for you since morning.

---

# 12. Since yesterday

> **Dünden beri hastayım.**

I've been sick since yesterday.

Breakdown:

**dün + den + beri**

---

# 13. Since Monday

> **Pazartesiden beri çalışıyorum.**

I've been working since Monday.

---

# 14. Since last year

> **Geçen yıldan beri Türkçe öğreniyorum.**

I've been learning Turkish since last year.

---

# 15. Since 2000

> **2000'den beri yurtdışında yaşıyorum.**

I've been living abroad since 2000.

Notice the apostrophe:

**2000'den**

Numbers take the suffix after an apostrophe.

More examples:

> **2020'den beri**

since 2020

> **2015'ten beri**

since 2015

> **1990'dan beri**

since 1990

---

# 16. Days/months/years can become plural

This is an interesting note from your book.

You can say:

> **Günlerden beri ders çalışıyor.**

I've been studying for days.

OR:

> **Günlerdir ders çalışıyor.**

I've been studying for days.

Same basic idea.

Another:

> **Yıllardan beri onunla arkadaşız.**

We've been friends with him/her for years.

=

> **Yıllardır onunla arkadaşız.**

We've been friends with him/her for years.

# 21. One important pronunciation/grammar detail

Look at:

> **23 Ekim'den beri**

Not:

❌ 23 Ekimdan beri

Because **Ekim** ends in a consonant and has a front vowel:

> Ekim + den → **Ekim'den**

Similarly:

> Ocak + tan → **Ocak'tan**

> Mart + tan → **Mart'tan**

> Mayıs + tan → **Mayıs'tan**

> Haziran + dan → **Haziran'dan**

# 🔥 Connect Units 5A + 5B + 5C

You're now seeing a whole **time system** developing:

### 5A — What time?

> **Saat kaç?**
> What time is it?

> **Saat üçü yirmi geçiyor.**
> It's 3:20.

### 5B — Before / after

> **Dersten sonra eve gidiyorum.**
> I go home after class.

> **Uyumadan önce kitap okuyorum.**
> I read before sleeping.

### 5C — Since / for

> **Üç aydır Türkçe öğreniyorum.**
> I've been learning Turkish for three months.

> **Hazirandan beri Türkçe öğreniyorum.**
> I've been learning Turkish since June.

So the three units together give you:

**WHEN? → WHAT TIME? → BEFORE/AFTER → SINCE/FOR**

That is a very useful A1 foundation for talking about your **daily routine, schedule, past-to-present activities, and how long you've been doing something**.
