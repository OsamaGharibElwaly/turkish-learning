
Here is a clear, beginner-friendly (A1) explanation for all 19 Turkish grammar rules you listed. I've ordered them in a way that builds logically from basic to more complex ideas.

---

### 1. Kişi Zamirleri — Personal Pronouns
These are the words for "I, you, he/she/it, we, you, they."

- **Ben** = I
- **Sen** = You (singular, informal)
- **O** = He / She / It
- **Biz** = We
- **Siz** = You (plural or formal singular)
- **Onlar** = They

**Key point:** Turkish pronouns have no gender. "O" means he, she, *or* it.

---

### 2. Şahıs Ekleri — Personal Endings
These are suffixes (endings) added to verbs or nouns to show *who* is doing the action or *who* the subject is. They match the personal pronouns.

**Example with the verb "gel-" (to come):**
- Ben gel**im** = I come
- Sen gel**sin** = You come
- O gel**ir** (no ending needed for 3rd person singular)
- Biz gel**iz** = We come
- Siz gel**siniz** = You come
- Onlar gel**ler** = They come

**For nouns** (to say "I am a student"):
- Ben öğrenci**yim** = I am a student.

---

### 3. Bu / Şu / O — This / That
These are demonstrative pronouns (pointing words).

- **Bu** = This (close to the speaker, physically or mentally).
- **Şu** = That (a bit further away, or to point out something specific in a story or conversation).
- **O** = That (far away) *and* he/she/it (as a pronoun).

**Example:**
- *Bu* araba güzel. (This car is nice – right next to me).
- *Şu* araba güzel. (That car is nice – over there, I'm pointing at it).
- *O* araba güzel. (That car is nice – far away).

---

### 4. Çoğul Eki (-lar / -ler) — Plural Suffix
This is how you make nouns plural (like -s in English). You choose **-lar** or **-ler** based on vowel harmony:

- **-lar** (after a, ı, o, u) – e.g., *araba* → **arabalar** (cars)
- **-ler** (after e, i, ö, ü) – e.g., *ev* → **evler** (houses)

**Important:** When using the plural suffix with a verb, you don't always need to repeat the plural ending on the verb (e.g., *Onlar geliyor* = They are coming – no -ler on the verb needed).

---

### 5. Var / Yok — There is / There isn't
These are used to express existence.

- **Var** = There is / There are / Have (possession).
- **Yok** = There isn't / There aren't / Don't have.

**Example:**
- *Masada kitap **var**.* = There is a book on the table.
- *Masada kitap **yok**.* = There isn't a book on the table.
- *Arabam **var**.* = I have a car. (Literally: My car exists).
- *Param **yok**.* = I don't have money.

---

### 6. Bulunma Hâli (-DA) — Locative Case
This suffix means **"in / on / at"**. It answers the question "Where?".

You choose the vowel and consonant:
- **-da** (after a, ı, o, u) – e.g., *okulda* = at school.
- **-de** (after e, i, ö, ü) – e.g., *evde* = at home.
- **-ta** (after voiceless consonants: f,s,t,k,ç,ş,h,p) – e.g., *dolapta* = in the cupboard.
- **-te** – e.g., *şehirde* = in the city.

---

### 7. İyelik Ekleri — Possessive Suffixes
These show ownership (like 's in English or "my, your, his").

| Subject | Suffix | Example: *ev* (house) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ben (my) | **-m / -im / -ım / -üm** | **evim** (my house) |
| Sen (your) | **-n / -in / -ın / -ün** | **evin** (your house) |
| O (his/her/its) | **-si / -sı / -su / -sü** | **evi** (his house) |
| Biz (our) | **-miz / -mız / -muz / -müz** | **evimiz** (our house) |
| Siz (your pl.) | **-niz / -nız / -nuz / -nüz** | **eviniz** (your house) |
| Onlar (their) | **-leri / -ları** | **evleri** (their house) |

---

### 8. Belirtme Hâli (-I) — Accusative Case
This marks the **direct object** of a verb, but **only when it is specific/definite** (like "the" in English).

- **-ı / -i / -u / -ü** (with consonant harmony) or **-yı / -yi / -yu / -yü** (after vowels).

**Example:**
- *Kitap okuyorum.* = I am reading **a** book. (No suffix – general).
- *Kitab**ı** okuyorum.* = I am reading **the** book. (With suffix – specific book you know about).

---

### 9. Yönelme Hâli (-A) — Dative Case
This suffix means **"to / towards"**. It shows direction.

You choose:
- **-a** (after a, ı, o, u) – e.g., *okula* = to school.
- **-e** (after e, i, ö, ü) – e.g., *eve* = to home.
- **-ya / -ye** (after vowels) – e.g., *Ankara'ya* = to Ankara.

**Example:** *Okul**a** gidiyorum.* = I am going **to** school.

---

### 10. Ayrılma Hâli (-DAn) — Ablative Case
This suffix means **"from"** or **"than"** (in comparisons).

You choose:
- **-dan / -den / -tan / -ten** based on vowel and consonant harmony (same rules as Locative, but with -n at the end).

**Example:**
- *Ev**den** çıktım.* = I left **from** home.
- *Okul**dan** geldim.* = I came **from** school.

---

### 11. Şimdiki Zaman (-yor) — Present Continuous Tense
This is the **"-ing"** tense in English (what is happening right now).

You add **-yor** to the verb root, then add the personal endings.

**Example with *gel-* (to come):**
- Ben gel**iyor**um = I am coming.
- Sen gel**iyor**sun = You are coming.
- O gel**iyor** = He is coming.

**Note:** Vowel changes happen before -yor (e.g., *bekle* → *bekliyor* = waiting).

---

### 12. Soru Eki (mI) — Question Particle
This makes yes/no questions (like "do you...?" or "are you...?").

It is written separately and takes vowel harmony:
- **mı / mi / mu / mü**

**Example:**
- *Okula gidiyor **musun**?* = Are you going to school? (Notice the personal ending attaches to the question particle).
- *Bu kitap **mı**?* = Is this a book?

---

### 13. Olumsuzluk (-mA) — Negation
This makes verbs negative (like "not" in English).

You add **-ma** or **-me** directly before the tense suffix.

**Example with *gel-* (to come):**
- *Gel**mi**yorum.* = I am **not** coming. (Notice *-ma* changes to *-mi* before -yor).
- *Gel**me**.* = Don't come! (Imperative).

---

### 14. Emir Kipi — Imperative Mood
This is used for commands or requests (like "Do it!" or "Don't do it!").

| Person | Suffix | Example (*gel-*) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Sen (you, singular) | (no ending) | **Gel!** (Come!) |
| Siz (you, plural/formal) | **-in / -ın / -un / -ün** | **Gelin!** (Come, please/you all!) |
| O (he/she/it) | **-sin / -sın / -sun / -sün** | **Gelsin!** (Let him come!) |
| Biz (we) | **-lim / -lım / -lum / -lüm** | **Geliyoruz!** (No, actually the imperative for "let's" is **-alım/-elim**: *Gelelim!* = Let's come! – sorry for the correction). |
| | **-in / -ın / -un / -ün** | **Gelin!** (You all come!) |

*(For clarity: The "let's" form uses **-alım / -elim**: *Gidelim!* = Let's go!)*

---

### 15. Geniş Zaman — Simple Present / Aorist
This tense is used for **habits**, **general truths**, and **routines** (like "I go to school every day").

The suffix is **-r / -ır / -ir / -ur / -ür** (and sometimes **-ar / -er**).

**Example with *gel-*:**
- Ben gel**ir**im = I come (every day / regularly).
- O gel**ir** = He comes (habitually).

**Negative** is made with **-mez / -maz**:
- O gel**mez** = He doesn't come (habitually).

---

### 16. İsim Tamlamaları — Noun Compounds
This is how you say "the door of the house" or "student's book" – linking two nouns.

There are two types:

1.  **Belirtili (Definite):** Possessor + Possessed.
    - *Evin kapısı* = The door of the house. (The first noun gets **-in**, the second gets **-i**).
2.  **Belirtisiz (Indefinite):** Noun + noun acting like a compound word.
    - *Kahvaltı* = breakfast (literally "coffee + before" – but more standard: *okul çantası* = school bag – first noun has no suffix, second gets **-i**).

---

### 17. Sıfatlar — Adjectives
Adjectives describe nouns (like "big," "red," "beautiful").

**In Turkish, adjectives ALWAYS come BEFORE the noun** and they do NOT change for plural or case.

**Example:**
- *Kırmızı araba* = Red car.
- *Kırmızı arabalar* = Red cars. (The adjective *kırmızı* stays the same).
- *Büyük ev* = Big house.
- *Büyük evde* = In the big house. (Adjective doesn't change).

---

### 18. Karşılaştırma: daha — Comparison with *daha*
This is how you say **"more"** or **"-er"** in English (e.g., bigger, more beautiful).

You put the word **daha** (meaning "more") **before** the adjective.

**Example:**
- *Büyük* = big. → *Daha büyük* = bigger.
- *Güzel* = beautiful. → *Daha güzel* = more beautiful.
- *Bu araba, o arabadan **daha** hızlı.* = This car is faster than that car. (Notice we use the Ablative case -dan for "than").

---

### 19. En + Sıfat — Superlative (*the most...*)
This is how you say **"the most"** or **"-est"** (e.g., biggest, most beautiful).

You put the word **en** (meaning "most") **before** the adjective.

**Example:**
- *Büyük* = big. → *En büyük* = the biggest.
- *Güzel* = beautiful. → *En güzel* = the most beautiful.
- *O, sınıfın **en** zeki öğrencisi.* = He is the most intelligent student in the class.

---

**Final A1 Tip:** The most important things to master first are **Vowel Harmony** (choosing the correct vowel in suffixes) and **Consonant Mutation** (when -d changes to -t, etc.). Every suffix rule above depends on those two core ideas!
